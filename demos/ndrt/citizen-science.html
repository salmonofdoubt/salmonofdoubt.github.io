Implementing Nature-Based Solutions

Each measure should answer the same field questions: purpose, fit, good function, warning signs, and what to record.

Riparian Buffer

  • Purpose: Slow runoff, trap sediment, reduce nutrient transfer, and support habitat continuity.
  • Where it fits: Stream or ditch margins with runoff pressure, bank vulnerability, or habitat opportunity.
  • Good function: Continuous vegetated strip, stable cover, low bare soil, limited trampling.
  • Record: Width, continuity, poaching, vegetation condition, bank damage, gaps.

Constructed Wetland

  • Purpose: Intercept nutrient-rich flows, slow water, store sediment, and support biodiversity.
  • Where it fits: Suitable interception points with enough space and manageable inflow.
  • Good function: Clear flow path, wetland vegetation, no major scour, no obvious bypass.
  • Record: Water presence, vegetation cover, blockage, erosion, bypass, litter.

Sediment Trap or Pond

  • Purpose: Capture coarse material and reduce downstream sediment transport.
  • Where it fits: Pathways where sediment movement is visible or expected.
  • Good function: Defined inlet and outlet, stored material where expected, no collapse.
  • Record: Fill level, overtopping, erosion, vegetation encroachment, maintenance need.

Hedgerow or Corridor Planting

  • Purpose: Support biodiversity, connectivity, shelter, and field-edge roughness.
  • Where it fits: Boundaries, edges, transitions, and corridor links.
  • Good function: Healthy growth, low dieback, limited browsing, clear continuity.
  • Record: Survival rate, gaps, guards, weed pressure, browsing, connectivity value.

Fencing and Stock Exclusion

  • Purpose: Protect banks, reduce trampling, and allow riparian recovery.
  • Where it fits: Livestock-access reaches with visible margin pressure.
  • Good function: Fence intact, access controlled, bank vegetation recovering.
  • Record: Fence condition, access points, poaching, exposed bank, regrowth.

Spartina and Estuary Stewardship

This is a distinct estuary issue. It should be monitored carefully and handled through guided review, not casual volunteer removal.

Why Spartina Matters

Spartina can alter marsh structure, sediment behaviour, access, and habitat condition. The right response is site-specific and should consider ecology, permissions, timing, and risk.

Identification first Site-specific judgement Supervised response

What Volunteers Should Not Do

  • Do not remove vegetation without guidance.
  • Do not assume every estuarine grass is Spartina.
  • Do not disturb protected habitat or nesting areas.
  • Do not work in unsafe tidal conditions.

Spot and Compare

Use a simple identification guide and photos before reporting a suspected patch.

02

Record the Sighting

Log location, date, photos, approximate extent, and nearby features such as creek edge, mudflat, or marsh vegetation.

03

Escalate to Guided Review

Any response should pass through a supervised review that considers habitat, permissions, timing, and ecological trade-offs.

CSSI Stream Health Checks

Keep this pathway simple, repeatable, and visually guided. It should welcome beginners without pretending to replace specialist methods.

What CSSI Is For

CSSI gives volunteers a structured way to observe stream condition, visible ecological signals, and broad indicator groups in a consistent manner.

Basic Field Kit

  • Phone or camera
  • Clipboard or waterproof field sheet
  • Polarised glasses if available
  • Tray, spoon, or net if training supports it
  • Boots, gloves, and site safety awareness

Traffic-light calendar for kick sampling

Use this as a precautionary guide for CSSI-style sampling in shallow stony riffles: shallow, gravelly or cobbly, fast-flowing stream sections. Local observations always override the calendar.

Current month: checking status
Red: do not kick sample

Avoid gravelly or stony fast-flowing areas when lamprey, salmonid redds, eggs, fry, or spawning signs may be present.

Amber: trained judgement only

Use visual checks first. Sample only if the reach is safe, non-sensitive, and no spawning signs are present.

Green: preferred window

Best general period for community CSSI checks, subject to flow, safety, land access, and local ecological conditions.

No-kick rule

If you see freshened gravel, nest-like depressions, adult lampreys, spawning fish, eggs, fry, or unusual fish activity, do not kick sample. Record the observation with photos and move the activity to visual survey only.

  • Visual-only alternatives include photos, habitat notes, algae cover, fine sediment, cattle access, outfalls, bank damage, flow observations, and basic water-quality readings where trained.
  • This is a community precaution guide, not a licence to enter private land, disturb protected habitat, or work in unsafe water conditions.
  • For formal Q-value assessment or sensitive sites, defer to EPA, LAWPRO, Inland Fisheries Ireland, NPWS, landowner, and trained-coordinator guidance.

Sources: CSSI field recording guidance from LAWPRO/UCC; Inland Fisheries Ireland lamprey species notes; Fisheries (Consolidation) Act 1959, section 173.

01

Choose a Reach

Select a safe, accessible section and note location, date, recent weather, and who is present.

02

Look at Habitat First

Observe clarity, banks, flow diversity, cover, substrate, shade, algae, fine sediment, and obvious disturbance.

03

Check the No-kick Rule

Before any kick sample, check for redds, freshened gravel, lamprey activity, eggs, fry, or spawning fish. If present, record visually only.

04

Observe Stream Life

Use the training guide to recognise broad groups rather than forcing false precision.

05

Score Consistently

Apply the same simple observation logic each time so results remain comparable across visits and hubs.

06

Submit Photos and Notes

Photos can be as valuable as numeric entries because they support review, interpretation, and follow-up.
