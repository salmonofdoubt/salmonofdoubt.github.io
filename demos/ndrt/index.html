A Journey Through Time

From the source near Brannanstown to the estuaries at Laytown, the river connects local heritage, ecological health, and the future of the catchment.

The River's Story

The Nanny flows through the heart of the landscape, passing through Kentstown, Duleek, and Julianstown. Along the way, it is joined by the River Hurley at Boolies Little before meeting the sea at the sheltered saltmarshes of Laytown.

Our 2028 Promise

We are not merely managing water. We are working to restore ecological integrity. By 2028, we aim to support measurable reductions in nutrient pressures through nature-based solutions that work with the land, including native hedgerows, wetlands, and riparian buffers.

Social Ecology

“Healthy ecosystems underpin a healthy society. By taking care of our river, we take care of our neighbours, our history, and the generations to come.”

Where Action Meets the Water

Restoration happens locally. The catchment is organised into six dedicated hubs so that each stream and community receives focused attention.

Local Action Hubs

From source control in the Delvin headwaters to invasive species management in the Nanny Estuary, local teams operate on the ground. The Trust supports coordination, funding pathways, and practical advice to make restoration repeatable and defensible.

The Nanny Watch (CSSI)

Join the River Guardian network. We use the Citizen Science Stream Index (CSSI) to help monitor river health. By observing stream life, habitat condition, and visible ecological signals, communities can better understand what the river needs to thrive.
