The Trust in One View

The homepage should establish credibility, show the restoration vision, and make it easy for people to understand where the Trust is working and why it matters.

The River's Story

The Nanny and Delvin system connects heritage, farms, villages, tributaries, wetlands, and estuarine habitats. The core message is simple: a healthier catchment supports biodiversity, resilience, and local identity.

Our 2028 Promise

The Trust is not merely reacting to decline. It is building a practical, community-facing programme of restoration through riparian work, wetland opportunities, habitat repair, invasive species response, and citizen monitoring.

Six Local Hubs, One Shared Direction

Restoration happens locally. The Trust can present the catchment as a set of linked action areas so that volunteers, landowners, and partner groups know where to plug in.

Headwaters and Source Areas

Early intervention matters. Source zones are where runoff pathways, drainage issues, and habitat condition can often be addressed before impacts accumulate downstream.

Middle Catchment Communities

Villages and farms are where practical stewardship becomes visible: fencing, buffers, wet features, habitat corridors, and shared observation of how the river behaves.

Estuary and Coastal Interface

The estuarine margin is where biodiversity, sediment dynamics, invasive species, public interest, and habitat management all converge.
