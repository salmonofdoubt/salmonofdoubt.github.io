The catchment at a glance

The site should orient first-time visitors quickly: where the catchment is, why it matters, and what makes it ecologically significant.

Catchment context

  • The Nanny-Delvin catchment includes waters draining to tidal water between Mornington Point and Sea Mount, County Dublin.
  • The catchment contains 6 sub-catchments, 34 river waterbodies, 3 transitional waters, 3 coastal waters, and 16 groundwater bodies.
  • The Nanny estuary, Rogerstown Estuary, and Malahide Estuary create a strong coastal-estuarine identity for the Trust.

Special conservation interests

  • River Nanny Estuary & Shore SPA and pNHA.
  • Rogerstown Estuary SAC, SPA, and pNHA.
  • Malahide Estuary SAC, SPA, and pNHA.
  • Strong importance for wintering wading birds, saltmarsh, mudflat, dune, and estuarine habitats.
64% Waterbodies recorded as at risk of not meeting environmental objectives.
Top pressure Agriculture is identified as the leading significant pressure in the catchment.
2028 Operational horizon for the community coordination framework and measurable restoration outcomes.

Strategic priorities

The homepage should express the Trust’s priorities exactly as the governing documents imply: ecological restoration, water quality, invasive species management, community action, and governance capacity.

Ecological restoration & biodiversity

  • Wetland ecosystem restoration
  • Ecosystem restoration planning for the Nanny Estuary
  • Extension of restoration objectives to Rogerstown and Malahide

Water quality

  • Monitoring and pollution prevention
  • Environmental database development
  • Holistic catchment collaboration with agencies and communities

Invasive species & community

  • Catchment-wide Spartina anglica coordination
  • Community action groups and member network
  • University and expert collaboration

Flagship and priority projects

These can become linked project pages later. For the prototype, they establish real momentum and strategic coherence.

Spartina management

A flagship catchment-wide initiative to manage and suppress common cord-grass through community action, ecological guidance, and public-body partnership.

Nanny Watch

A citizen-science stream for monthly nitrate, phosphate, and turbidity observations, public learning, and adaptive management.

Nature-based solutions deployment

Riparian buffers, sediment traps, wetlands, hedgerows, leaky barriers, and rewetting measures organised as practical local interventions.

Operational structure

Coordination
The Trust oversees implementation, reporting, and stakeholder engagement.
Community hub
Sonairte serves as meeting space, demonstration venue, and outreach platform.
Farm advice
Teagasc ASSAP supports Rainwater Management Plans and funding linkage.
Technical oversight
LAWPRO aligns actions with WFD objectives and supports mapping, data, and training.

Sub-catchment logic

  • Implementation is organised across six EPA-recognised sub-catchments.
  • Teams combine local landholder representation, technical liaison, and community volunteers.
  • Action should be matched to hydrological role, nutrient pressure, and opportunity for stacked nature-based solutions.
  • Representative farms, fixed monitoring points, and annual review support adaptive management.
