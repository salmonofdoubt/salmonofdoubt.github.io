The River's Story

From headwaters to estuary, the catchment connects landscapes, communities, habitats, and water. The main page should establish who the Trust is, why it matters, and how people can enter the work.

Why This Trust Exists

The Trust exists to support practical restoration, strengthen stewardship, and turn concern for river health into visible action. Healthy rivers support biodiversity, local identity, landscape resilience, and long-term community benefit.

Catchment-scale thinking Nature-based action Community stewardship

Our 2028 Promise

By 2028, the Trust aims to support visible, defensible progress through river corridor care, wetland opportunities, habitat repair, estuary stewardship, and citizen science. The public site should inspire trust. The action site should enable participation.

Six Local Hubs, One Shared Direction

Restoration happens locally. A hub structure helps people see where they belong and how the wider catchment fits together.

Headwaters and Source Areas

Upstream intervention matters. Source areas are often where runoff pathways, drainage pressures, and habitat condition can be addressed before impacts intensify downstream.

Middle Catchment Communities

Farms, villages, and field margins are where stewardship becomes visible through buffers, wetlands, hedgerows, fencing, and repeat site observation.

Estuary and Coastal Interface

The estuarine edge is where habitat, biodiversity, sediment dynamics, invasive species, and public interest all converge.

Healthy ecosystems underpin healthy communities.
