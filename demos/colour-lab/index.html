Additive light and subtractive paint

This demo separates two different physical situations: RGB light on a white screen, and paint on a white palette or canvas under white illumination.

In the first case, the screen reflects the incident RGB mixture. In the second, the pigments absorb part of the white light and reflect the remainder toward the eye.

Back to Demos RGB Light Paint Mixing

Interpretation

Light on a white screen is additive in effect. Paint under white light is subtractive in effect.

The paint section is an educational approximation using absorbance-style mixing in linear RGB. It is much better than naïve RGB averaging, but it is not a full spectral pigment model.

What the demo is saying

In the light section, the colour reaching the eye is the incident RGB mixture scaled by the reflectance of the white screen.

In the paint section, the colour reaching the eye is the reflected remainder of the white illumination after pigment absorption. The model here is intentionally approximate, but it is conceptually much closer to paint behaviour than averaging display RGB values.
