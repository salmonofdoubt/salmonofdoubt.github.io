Additive light and artist-mode paint mixing

This demo separates two different physical situations: RGB light on a white screen, and paint on a white palette or canvas under white illumination.

The light section shows what a neutral white screen reflects into the eye. The paint section uses named artist colours and an approximate subtractive model to estimate what the painted surface reflects under white light.

Back to Demos RGB Light Artist Mode

Interpretation

Light on a white screen is additive in effect. Paint under white light is subtractive in effect.

Artist mode uses named oil-paint style presets. The paint section is an educational approximation using absorbance-style mixing in linear RGB. It is far more painterly than naïve RGB averaging, but it is not a full spectral pigment model.

What the demo is saying

In the light section, the colour reaching the eye is the incident RGB mixture scaled by the reflectance of the white screen.

In the artist mode section, the colour reaching the eye is the reflected remainder of the white illumination after pigment-like absorption. The model is intentionally approximate, but it is conceptually much closer to paint behaviour than averaging display RGB values.
