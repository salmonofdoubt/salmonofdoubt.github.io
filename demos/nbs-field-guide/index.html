Field principle

An NbS is not simply a green object placed in a landscape. Its performance emerges from hydrology, soils, vegetation, organisms, maintenance, governance, and time.

Start with pressure → pathway → function → evidence.
Working definition. UNEA Resolution 5/5 defines NbS as actions that protect, conserve, restore, sustainably use, and manage natural or modified ecosystems while addressing societal challenges and providing human well-being, ecosystem-service, resilience, and biodiversity benefits.

02 · Monitoring logic

Baseline → pathway → response → adapt.

Monitoring becomes useful when it tests the mechanism behind the intervention, not merely whether the intervention still exists.

1

Baseline

Record the pre-intervention condition, hydrology, seasonality, pressures, and relevant context.

2

Pressure + pathway

Confirm how the pressure is generated, mobilised, transported, and delivered to the receptor.

3

Response indicator

Measure a process or outcome that can reveal whether the intended function is actually occurring.

4

Adapt

Inspect, maintain, modify, relocate, or stop when the evidence contradicts the original assumption.

Field rule: pair at least one implementation indicator (condition, vegetation, hydraulic function) with at least one outcome indicator (water quality, flow response, habitat, or other target response) where proportionate.

04 · Limits and trade-offs

Green does not mean consequence-free.

Good NbS practice makes uncertainty, maintenance burden, land-use implications, and possible adverse effects explicit before implementation.

Right measure, right place

Hydrological connectivity and landscape context can determine whether an intervention intercepts the pressure at all.

Local ≠ catchment-scale

A visible local effect should not automatically be translated into a large downstream or catchment-wide benefit.

Maintenance is part of performance

Sediment accumulation, blocked structures, vegetation change, and damaged buffers can change function through time.

Co-benefits need evidence too

Biodiversity, carbon, amenity, drought, and flood claims should be treated as testable propositions, not decorative labels.

Demo boundary. This guide supports early-stage thinking and field discussion. It is not engineering design, flood-risk advice, ecological assessment, regulatory approval, or a substitute for site-specific professional judgement.
