Rotational geometry laboratory

Sphere Machine

Take a cube, pyramid, torus, sphere, or an almost two-dimensional plate. Rotate it about the axes you choose. As RPM rises, many orientations are integrated into one visible form.

At low speed you can still see the individual object. At high speed the demo overlays many complete orientations of that same object, approximating the simultaneous-view idea in Spherism.

What is actually happening?

Many orientations become one visible form.

For any point fixed in the object, rotation changes its direction from the centre but not its distance from the centre. Its possible positions therefore lie on a sphere of that radius.

At high RPM the renderer shows many complete orientations of the geometry over the same visible moment. The separate profiles progressively merge into a rounded time-integrated form, while the outer trace measures how completely the extreme points have sampled direction.

Instantaneous vs integrated form

The sphere is the synthesis.

At any single instant a cube is still geometrically a cube. The sphere-like appearance in this experiment is the simultaneous visual integration of many orientations.

That is the part that corresponds most directly to the paper’s Infinite Camera thought experiment: many partial perspectives are brought together rather than viewed one after another.

The compact mathematics

A rotation cannot change distance from the origin.

‖R(t)x‖² = xᵀR(t)ᵀR(t)x = xᵀx = ‖x‖²

Each material point therefore remains on a spherical shell determined by its radius. The visual question is how completely the chosen rotational programme samples orientation during the integrated exposure.

Concept reference

From proposition to experiment

This demonstrator is an interactive companion to André Baumann’s Spherism: Seeing the Sphere as Reality Beyond Appearances. Its high-RPM mode approximates the paper’s Infinite Camera intuition by overlaying many complete orientations of an object into one visible synthesis.
