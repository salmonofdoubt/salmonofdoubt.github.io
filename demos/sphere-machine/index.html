The useful bridge

Sphere Machine averages classical occupancies across orientations. Quantum mechanics assigns probabilities from a state and, for pure states, from amplitudes through the Born rule. The connection is symmetry + probability + averaging; the mechanisms are not identical.

4 · Why it works

The mathematics, after you have seen the effect.

A · Building the sphere

Average the object over rotations.

Let B be the object and 1B(x) its indicator: 1 inside the object and 0 outside.

PS(x) = ∫SO(3) 1B(R−1x) dμ(R)

Because the rotation measure is invariant, PS(Qx) = PS(x) for every rotation Q. A fully averaged field can therefore depend only on radius:

PS(x) = p(r),   r = ‖x‖
B · Borrowing from QM

Probability and symmetry averaging—not quantum identity.

For a quantum density operator ρ:

PQ(x) = ⟨x|ρ|x⟩

A rotationally averaged quantum state is:

ρ̄ = ∫SO(3) U(R)ρU(R)† dμ(R)

Quantum superposition additionally contains complex amplitude, phase and interference. Sphere Machine does not simulate those.

Why does the equal 2p mixture become spherical?
ρ2p,mix = ⅓ Σm=−11 |2,1,m⟩⟨2,1,m|
Σm=−ll |Ylm(θ,φ)|² = (2l+1)/(4π)

The spherical-harmonic addition theorem makes the angular sum independent of direction. Individual 2p states can be directional; the equal incoherent mixture across the full m multiplet is isotropic.

Hydrogen 1s and 2s reference
|ψ1s(r)|² ∝ e−2r/a₀
|ψ2s(r)|² ∝ (2 − r/a₀)²e−r/a₀

The 2s state is still spherically symmetric but has a radial node at r = 2a₀. Spherical symmetry therefore does not imply uniform or featureless probability.
