Spherism · a thought experiment in perspective

Sphere Machine

Look at an object from one point. Then another. Now imagine an octopus wrapping sensor-covered arms around it — and finally, every possible viewpoint at once. Superimpose those observations. What seemed definitive from one position begins to lose its privilege.

What remains when no single perspective is allowed to define reality?

one view → appearance many views → challenge evidence → what survives

The cube is only the beginning. The same question reaches people, processes, opinions and public narratives. More perspectives do not make every claim true; they make each claim answerable to others — and to evidence.

3 · The larger idea

A perspective can be true and still be incomplete.

A cube can look square from one position and hexagonal from another. Neither view is false; neither is the whole object. The same caution applies to people, processes, opinions, histories and public narratives.

Democracy works best when no government, party, institution, expert or majority can define the whole without challenge from other perspectives and evidence. Propaganda does the opposite: it tries to make one projection feel like reality itself.

The idea is also close to Zen: do not mistake the frame, category or story your mind has settled on for the thing itself. More perspectives do not make every claim equally true; they make claims testable against one another and against evidence.

What survives that process of challenge may be closer to the invariant structure underneath appearances.

Sphere Machine averages classical occupancies across orientations. Quantum mechanics assigns probabilities from quantum states and, for pure states, from amplitudes through the Born rule. The connection is symmetry + probability + averaging; a rotating cube is not a quantum superposition.

5 · Why the sphere appears

The mathematics underneath the metaphor.

A · Removing directional privilege

Average the object over rotations.

Let B be the object and 1B(x) its indicator: 1 inside the object and 0 outside.

PS(x) = ∫SO(3) 1B(R−1x) dμ(R)

Because the rotation measure is invariant, PS(Qx) = PS(x) for every rotation Q. Once no direction is privileged, the fully averaged field can depend only on radius:

PS(x) = p(r),   r = ‖x‖

This does not mean every object literally becomes a solid sphere. A torus, for example, can approach a spherical outer envelope while retaining p(0)=0.

B · Where the analogy stops

Probability and symmetry averaging—not quantum identity.

For a quantum density operator ρ:

PQ(x) = ⟨x|ρ|x⟩

A rotationally averaged quantum state is:

ρ̄ = ∫SO(3) U(R)ρU(R)† dμ(R)

Quantum superposition additionally contains complex amplitude, phase and interference. Sphere Machine does not simulate those.

Why does the equal 2p mixture become spherical?
ρ2p,mix = ⅓ Σm=−11 |2,1,m⟩⟨2,1,m|
Σm=−ll |Ylm(θ,φ)|² = (2l+1)/(4π)

The spherical-harmonic addition theorem makes the angular sum independent of direction. Individual 2p states can be directional; the equal incoherent mixture across the full m multiplet is isotropic.

Hydrogen 1s and 2s reference
|ψ1s(r)|² ∝ e−2r/a₀
|ψ2s(r)|² ∝ (2 − r/a₀)²e−r/a₀

The 2s state is still spherically symmetric but has a radial node at r = 2a₀. Spherical symmetry therefore does not imply uniform or featureless probability.
