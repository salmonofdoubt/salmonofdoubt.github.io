River analogy

The map chooses a place. The fractal explores possible futures.

Select an illustrative reach, then apply a small storm pulse or intervention. The marker moves through the mathematical outcome map. Crossing a coloured edge changes the final outcome.

Important distinction

The Google map and reach names do not supply scientific model data. This remains an educational analogy.

How to read the graphic

Three things only

  1. 1
    Every pixel is a starting condition.

    Its colour tells you which final outcome it reaches.

  2. 2
    Solid colour means relative predictability.

    Nearby points generally reach the same outcome.

  3. 3
    The coloured lace is the fractal edge.

    Near it, tiny changes can switch the outcome.

Possible SDSS extension

Keep the interface, replace the toy mathematics

A later SDSS version could replace Newton's equation with a calibrated nonlinear river model. The interface could then examine recovery basins, intervention robustness and proximity to undesirable outcome boundaries.

Current limitation

This prototype demonstrates fractal basin logic. It does not establish that the Nanny, Delvin or any Irish river has a fractal ecological basin boundary.
