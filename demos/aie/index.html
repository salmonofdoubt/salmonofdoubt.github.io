Current route in one minute
  1. Initial request: ask the relevant public authority for environmental information held by or for it.
  2. Internal review: if the initial decision is unsatisfactory or the request was not properly dealt with, Article 11 provides a fresh review by the authority. This is normally the necessary step before OCEI.
  3. OCEI appeal: if a genuine access issue remains after internal review, appeal to the Commissioner under Article 12. OCEI's online form is the easiest route; its published information also permits appeals by email to info@ocei.ie or by post.

Important limits

AIE is an information-access route, not the decision on the underlying project.

Preserve the baseline.

An initial decision records what the authority said it held, found, released or refused at that point. It is not automatically proof that an assessment never happened.

Located later ≠ created later.

If information appears on review, record when it was actually created and whether it existed at the date of the original request.

Review has a trade-off.

Internal review can improve disclosure and can also improve the authority's search and documentary organisation. Preserve both stages.

Use the right forum.

Use AIE evidence in consultation, research, regulation, representation or professional advice as appropriate. OCEI reviews information-access decisions, not whether the underlying project should proceed.
