Method note

How this radar works

The UrbanForest Radar is a practical intelligence layer for small urban forest and UrbanForest delivery. It prioritises usefulness over exhaustive coverage.

Operating principle

Survival beyond the launch photo

Items are ranked by whether they help someone design, fund, plant, maintain, monitor, defend, or communicate an intervention.

Radar structure

Five practical lanes

Ireland Urban Forest Practice

Irish delivery, campuses, schools, communities, local authorities, and practical implementation.

Transferable UrbanForest Practice

SuDS, rain gardens, bioswales, shade, depaving, soil restoration, and UrbanForest delivery.

Funding and Opportunities

Grants, schemes, calls, and support routes for planting, monitoring, maintenance, education, and biodiversity.

Practical Research and Evidence

Evidence for biodiversity, wellbeing, heat, shade, survival, maintenance, monitoring, soil, and governance.

Design, Maintenance and Risk

Watering, drought stress, soil care, vandalism, public acceptance, carbon claims, and long-term stewardship risk.

Ranking logic

How usefulness is estimated

The score is a prioritisation aid, not a truth claim. It favours Dublin/Ireland relevance, practical delivery value, maintenance/survival risk, funding fit, research usefulness, and transferable temperate-city evidence.

Watched benefit and delivery categories

Urban heat / shade, biodiversity / habitat, soil health, stormwater / SuDS, wellbeing / health, school / education, community stewardship, tree survival / maintenance, funding / grants, planning / policy, monitoring / evaluation, carbon / climate claims, and risk / safety.

Known limitations

Google News RSS can miss items, source pages change structure, grants require manual deadline checks, and research usefulness depends on metadata and abstracts. The radar supports judgement; it does not replace it.
