## Reference sources
- Lesley Logan, [Roll Over on the Mat](https://onlinepilatesclasses.com/pilates-exercises/mat-exercises/mat-roll-over/) — specifically identifies the exercise as **advanced**; describes arms at sides, legs reaching overhead and possibility of toes approaching/touching the mat; cautions against neck issues and dropping legs onto the torso.
- Joseph H. Pilates and William J. Miller, *Return to Life Through Contrology* (1945), exercise 3, *The Roll-Over With Legs Spread (Both Ways)*; text consulted through a 2012 revised digital transcription. Sequence: flat supine, arms alongside body with palms down; straight legs lift overhead, toes reaching mat; arms press into mat; legs separate and spine returns slowly, then repeat in reverse configuration. The historical original describes full toe contact, but contemporary instructors caution **not to force** this if control or neck safety is compromised.
- Birmingham Pilates Studios, [Return to Life: #3 Roll Over](https://www.birminghampilatesstudios.com/on-the-mat-roll-over/) — stresses shoulders rather than neck as the intended support; acknowledges that reaching toes to the ground may not be possible.

## Coordinate convention — non-negotiable
Camera fixed in SIDE PROFILE. Head always on the LEFT of every image, feet to the RIGHT at the initial supine position. Never turn or mirror the mannequin, scene or camera halfway through a movement.
Head and shoulders remain at the SAME spot on the mat while pelvis and legs travel upward and over to the LEFT. Legs connect at the hip sockets embedded in the pelvis. The pelvis moves with the spine: no detached / reversed buttocks.

## Classical movement phases for engineering
A. **Pose 1 / horizontal start:** face up, entire back on the mat, legs close together and extended right; ankles in a plausible pointed-toe (plantarflexed) orientation; arms long alongside torso, palms grounded. Head left.
B. **Leg lift:** legs raise together; hips flex while the pelvis begins a controlled posterior roll. Head and shoulders remain grounded. Any knee bend here would be a distinct modification, not silently labelled original.
C. **Overhead phase:** progressively raise sacrum, then lumbar and lower thoracic spine. Pelvis is above and toward the head, connected to the trunk by a continuous spinal curve. Weight is controlled by the shoulder girdle/upper back and arms, **not by the top of the head or by the cervical spine**. Extended legs approach or—if the athlete can do so without forcing—the floor behind the head, which is to the LEFT. Feet remain joined and anatomically oriented; no 180° backward ankle.
D. **Separate / roll down:** spread the legs as prescribed; control the spinal return to supine and bring legs down with correct hip connection. The rolling-back-down frame is NOT equivalent to a still of the feet touching the mat behind the head.

## Anatomic reference landmark checklist
- Ovoid head sits on neck, which is continuously attached to thoracic spine.
- Thoracic spine transitions continuously through lumbar region to sacrum and pelvis; no sharp artificial bend above sacrum.
- Both femoral heads connect to the left/right hip sockets in pelvis; no femur penetrating torso or emerging from spine.
- The buttocks/pelvis are lifted overhead. Their relative position is anatomically credible—not visually exchanged with abdomen.
- Both legs remain extended and show plausible hip flexion; knees flex/extend only on their axis.
- Both feet attach via ankle joints, toes oriented away from heel in line with the foot. Plantarflexion is NOT a detached pointed shape. Floor contact, when shown, should be the toes behind the head without forced neck compression.
- Arms remain on the mat beside the body, palms down, shoulder blades stable. No hand-held lower back for the original classical Roll Over.
- Shoulder girdle and upper back support the lifted torso. Avoid depicting body weight on the skull or compressed neck.
- Fixed external scenery, light, shadows and camera perspective through all frames.
